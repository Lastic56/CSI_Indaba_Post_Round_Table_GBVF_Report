Beyond Awareness: A Responsibility to Act

The CSI Indaba Post Round-Table GBVF Report. Connecting dialogue, historical cost research, and practical directions for corporate partnerships.

Download

Gender-Based Violence and Femicide (GBVF) challenges the society we are prepared to build. While economic losses strengthen the business case, human dignity remains the reason to act.

This report moves beyond traditional awareness campaigns. It outlines a practical response for corporate South Africa: sustained prevention, stronger care systems, and leadership willing to examine everyday power. To turn intent into delivery, CSI Indaba is launching the Women, Children and Gender Office to coordinate corporate CSI contributions and connect investment with specialist, frontline delivery.

Key Findings

The 6 Strategic Shifts

The Paradigm: Moving corporate CSI from Awareness → Prevention, Wellness → Protection, Projects → Continuity, Adults → Early Support, Silos → Coordination, and Activity → Outcomes.

The Goal: Fund work that changes harmful norms and back capable community organisations with realistic operating support.

The Economic Reality

The Statistic: An estimated >R36 billion national economic loss attributed to GBV in 2019.

The Hidden Cost: R9.96 billion in estimated out-of-pocket medical costs for survivors seeking help (55% physicians, 31.8% prescription meds, 13.2% travel/other).

The 4 Priorities for Action

PREVENT: Invest earlier in children, caregivers, and safer norms.

PROTECT: Recognise abuse and connect people with specialist support.

PARTNER: Sustain the organisations closest to communities.

MEASURE: Track access, service quality, and safety-related outcomes.

The Economic Case & Cost of Care

The Numbers Discussed: Significant, but Historical. Note: These are historical national estimates, not a 2026 corporate audit.

  • > R36 Billion: Estimated national economic loss attributed to GBV in 2019 (Human capital loss R26bn, Judicial costs R104m, Out-of-pocket medical ~R9.96bn).
  • The 10% Illustrative Scenario: A transparent mathematical exercise shows that a 10% annual compounding assumption on the R36bn anchor implies an illustrative 2026 value of R70.15bn.
  • International Context: The UK Home Office repriced its 2016/17 domestic-abuse estimate from £66bn to £74bn (a 12.1% cumulative price uplift).

Understanding Prevention

Look at Interacting Conditions, Not a Single Cause. An editorial synthesis informed by the WHO and CDC risk-and-protection frameworks:

  • SOCIETY: Gender inequality, norms that excuse violence, weak accountability.
  • COMMUNITY: Service access, economic conditions, safe spaces, trusted support.
  • RELATIONSHIPS: Control, household power, caregiving, conflict, social isolation.
  • INDIVIDUAL: Experiences of violence, wellbeing, beliefs, access to care.

CSI Implication: Build protection at every level. Risk factors are not destiny; responsibility for violence remains with the person who uses it.

Inside the Company: Workplace Protection

Make Workplace Protection Part of Corporate Strategy. Proposed practice agenda for HR, CSI, leadership, and specialist partners:

Situation or Gap Practical Response Accountable Function
Employee appears distressed Offer private, supportive conversation. Avoid assumptions or demands for disclosure. Trained line manager / HR
Employee discloses abuse Ask what support is wanted; discuss safe contact and specialist referral. Explain confidentiality limits. HR + safeguarding lead
Wellness is the only response Review referral quality and access. Agree safe workplace accommodations case by case. HR + service provider
Risk affects work environment Develop tailored workplace safety plan with employee and qualified services. HR + security + specialist
Board sees only campaigns Report readiness, referral performance, and anonymised service-quality measures alongside investment. Executive sponsor + board

Funding for Continuity

Fund the Capacity That Makes Protection Possible. Addressing the dialogue's challenge to short grants and operating-cost restrictions:

  • Challenge: Short project cycles interrupt care. ➔ Response: Offer multi-year agreements with proportionate reviews.
  • Challenge: Operating costs are treated as peripheral. ➔ Response: Assess full delivery costs (staff, supervision, safeguarding, transport).
  • Challenge: Small orgs carry heavy reporting burdens. ➔ Response: Use shared reporting templates or a capable intermediary.

The Practical Test: Does the grant strengthen the service people need, or only produce a report the funder can count?

The Women, Children & Gender Office

Programme Focus & The Corporate Pledge. CSI Indaba is launching this office to coordinate the corporate CSI contribution to GBVF prevention and response.

The 3-Programme Framework:

  • Immediate Response: Practical help when a family needs it most (burial essentials, urgent food/toiletries, safe-accommodation referrals).
  • Aftercare & Healing: Psychosocial support, grief counselling, and trauma-informed support for bereaved families and survivors.
  • Prevention & Family Wellbeing: Act earlier to reduce future harm by supporting caregivers, schools, and workplaces to recognise abuse and challenge harmful norms.

The Corporate Pledge (From ZAR 50,000 Upwards): We recognise that preventing GBVF is a shared responsibility. We commit to:

  • RESPOND FIRST: Support burial essentials and urgent family needs.
  • SUPPORT HEALING: Enable qualified psychosocial and grief support.
  • PREVENT HARM: Invest in evidence-informed prevention programmes.
  • SUSTAIN CARE: Back capable frontline partners.
  • PARTNER: Appoint an executive sponsor and work with CSI Indaba.
  • ACCOUNT: Track commitments and report progress without identifying families.

The 90-Day Roadmap

Turning Intent Into Delivery. An indicative timetable from programme approval:

  • Days 01–30 (PREPARE): Establish office lead, assess partner capacity, agree referral arrangements, cost burial essentials, and invite corporate pledges.
  • Days 31–60 (ACTIVATE): Confirm resources received, assess referred families, arrange practical assistance and psychosocial referrals, agree budgets.
  • Days 61–90 (REVIEW): Review response times and assistance delivered, follow up with families safely, begin prevention activities, and report aggregate progress.
